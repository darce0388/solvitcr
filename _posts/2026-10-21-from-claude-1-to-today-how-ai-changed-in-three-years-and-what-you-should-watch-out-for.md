I'll be honest with you: a good part of this website, the description of every service, and even the way I handle each new client were all built with help from artificial intelligence. Specifically Claude, the assistant made by Anthropic. So it felt right to make AI the topic of the first post on this blog.

I'm not here to sell you AI as if it were magic. I want to tell you how it got here, what it's actually useful for, and what I would never do with it.

## 2023: The one almost nobody knew about

Claude launched in March 2023, and back then it was only available to a handful of companies that had requested access. If you weren't a developer, you probably never heard of it.

In July of that year, Claude 2 came out and was opened to the public. It was handy for drafting an email or summarizing a document, but you had to double-check everything. It had a bad habit of making things up with total confidence, and for someone who works in tech support, that's a red flag.

## 2024: It started to see and write better

In March 2024, Anthropic released the Claude 3 family in three versions: Haiku, the fast one; Sonnet, the middle option; and Opus, the most capable. Anthropic still uses that structure today. Claude could also read images for the first time. You could send it a screenshot of an error message and it would explain what was going on.

In June came Claude 3.5 Sonnet, and for a lot of people who work with computers, that was the turning point. It stopped being a curiosity and became a real work tool.

## 2025: From talking to doing

This is where things changed. With Claude 3.7 Sonnet in February and Claude 4 in May, AI stopped being just a chat window. Claude Code arrived, which writes and fixes software directly on a developer's computer, and web search was added so Claude could look up current information instead of relying only on what it already knew.

## 2026: Where it is today

This has been the busiest year yet. In April, Anthropic introduced Claude Mythos Preview, a model so good at finding security flaws in software that the company decided not to release it to the public. Only selected organizations use it, through a program called Project Glasswing, to fix vulnerabilities before someone else can exploit them. When an AI company chooses to hold back a model because of what it could do in the wrong hands, that tells you a lot about where we are.

In June came Claude Fable 5, the first model of that new class available to the public, with extra safeguards. And on September 22, Anthropic released Claude Opus 5.5, which the company says performs at the level of Claude Fable 5.1 on most tasks and costs about 40% less to run than the previous Opus.

Put simply: what was an experiment for developers in 2023 can now read long documents, analyze data, put together presentations, and work on a task for hours while you do something else.

## What I use it for (and what I don't)

At SolvIT, I use it to write and review content in English and Spanish, organize my pricing and processes, and maintain this website. It saves me hours every week.

What I never do: give it passwords, account numbers, ID documents, or any of my clients' information. I also don't let it make a technical decision without reviewing it myself first. AI can be wrong with complete confidence, and I'm still the one responsible for the work.

## What you should watch out for

The same technology that helps us is also being used by scammers. The fake emails and messages going around Costa Rica now arrive well written, without the spelling mistakes that used to give them away. A big part of that change comes from AI.

Four basic rules:

1. **Only download apps from the official source.** Claude is available at [claude.ai](http://claude.ai) and in the official Apple and Google app stores. There are fake copies out there that ask for payments or personal information.
2. **Don't paste anything into an AI chat that you wouldn't hand to a stranger:** passwords, bank statements, or photos of your passport or residency card.
3. **Be suspicious of urgency, even if the message is perfectly written.** Good English or good Spanish doesn't prove anything anymore.
4. **Verify before you act.** If an AI tells you to change something on your computer or your router, check with someone who knows before you do it.

AI is here to stay, and when it's used well it saves a huge amount of time. But it still needs someone on the other side who knows what they're doing.
